How Squall reviews an app.

The full review checks your code and your live app in one run.

The review, step by step.

Permission

A signed permission names the repository and every hostname. Anything outside it stops the run.

Secrets in history

Two scanners read the full history of the code. A key proven to be live is rated High.

Flawed packages

Every package with a known flaw, and the version that fixes it.

Code analysis

Rules for your language and framework, plus a search for patterns that turn user input into code.

Site settings

The security headers your site sends, including the content security policy.

Where your data goes

Every host your app can reach, and what a real session sends and stores in the browser.

Changes on a preview

A proposed settings change runs on a preview first, so it cannot break the live app.

Hostile input

Crafted inputs run through the app's own parsing, offline.

Account settings

Read-only checks on the accounts that can publish your app.

A person decides

Every unproven item is confirmed or cleared with a reason.

The rules.

Permission firstNothing is touched without a signed permission naming it. Third-party services are observed, never probed.
Read-onlySquall changes nothing in your code, deployment or accounts.
No disruptionOne request at a time. No load testing, no fuzzing of live hosts.
No peopleNo phishing, no calls, no attempts on your staff.
Every run recordedOne line per run: what was asked, under which permission, and the outcome.
PrivateFindings go only to you. Nothing is published.
Not certificationNot a replacement for testing a regulator requires, and not a certification.

The report.

Each finding comes with its proof, why it matters and the fix, ranked by severity. The report ends with what passed and what was not covered.

CriticalConfirmed and reachable: data theft or full compromise.
HighSerious and exploitable with modest effort.
MediumA real weakness that needs conditions to exploit.
LowA minor gap with limited impact.
InfoAn observation with no direct risk.
UnverifiedPlausible but not proven. The missing proof is named.
