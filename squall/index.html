A report you can check, line by line.

Most scans hand you a list and a score. Squall hands you the proof behind each item, a person's judgement on what is real, and a plain account of where the review stopped.

Evidence on every finding

The commit, the header, the host, the line. A finding without proof is labelled unverified and says exactly which proof is missing.

What it could not check, in writing

Every report ends with what passed and what was out of reach, named, with the reason. You know the edges of the review before you rely on it.

A person reads it before you do

Automation does the checking. A human decides what is real: confirmed, or cleared with a written reason. Raw scanner output never reaches you unread.

From request to report in six steps.

Ownership and scope come first. Nothing runs until both are in place, and nothing runs outside them.

01

Prove ownership

A DNS TXT record on your domain, or read access to your repo.

02

Sign the scope

Every host and repo, the checks allowed, the dates.

03

Automated review

Read-only, human pace, raw output kept for every step.

04

Human read

Each flagged item confirmed or cleared with a reason.

05

Report

Delivered privately. Never published.

06

Nightly watch (optional)

Re-runs on new commits. You hear only when findings change.

Quiet until something changes.

With nightly watch, Squall re-reviews every new commit and compares the result with the last run. Same findings, no email. Something new, a report.

Built with limits, on purpose.

A security review should never become the incident. These hold on every engagement.

Owner-verified only

No target is touched without proven ownership and a signed scope. Third-party services are observed as traffic, never probed.

No disruption

No load testing, no fuzzing of live hosts, no attempt to take anything down.

No social engineering

No phishing, no calls, no attempts on your people.

Read-only

Squall recommends. It changes nothing in your code, deployment or accounts.

Squall is not a replacement for accredited testing a regulator requires, and not a compliance attestation or certification.
