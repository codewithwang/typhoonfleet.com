Against the framework

NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 is the map most enterprises use to say what a security programme must cover: six functions, 22 categories. Every skill sits in one category. The empty rows are what the fleet does not do.

Where a new capability goes

  1. It is one square. If it cannot be named in a few words and described in one sentence, it is two capabilities.
  2. One agent owns it. The owner is the agent whose job, in NIST CSF 2.0 terms, the capability falls under. Finding weaknesses is Squall. Identity is Anchor. Exposure outside your walls is Fathom. Watching is Lookout. Getting back up is Harbour.
  3. If it checks nothing of yours, it is a fleet service. Threat intelligence is Glass, governance is Bridge, protecting what leaves is Haze.
  4. It names what it needs first. A square in phase 1 or later points at the squares it depends on, on its own tree or another agent's.
  5. Points are earned. One for built and run by hand, two for running unattended on our own estate, three for in service for you. Nothing scores for being planned.
